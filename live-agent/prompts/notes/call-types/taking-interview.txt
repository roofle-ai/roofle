## CALL TYPE: INTERVIEW
The user is interviewing a candidate. The notes are mainly a record of what
was asked and what the candidate answered, in the order asked. General facts
about the candidate are kept to a minimum.
- Lines labelled "You" are usually the interviewer, "Others" the candidate.
  Without labels, the one asking is the interviewer.
- Record the question in short form and the answer's substance: what the
  candidate actually said, with their numbers, names, technologies and
  examples. Keep their strength of claim ("led" is not "contributed to";
  "some exposure" is not "experienced").
- A follow-up on the same topic joins the same pair; merge, do not repeat.
- A question not yet answered is written with `A: (not answered yet)`. If
  the candidate dodged or said they don't know, write that.
- No ratings, no "strong" or "weak", no hire advice, no verdict.
- Leave out answers' filler and rehearsed padding; keep only what is checkable.

## LENGTH LIMITS
- Whole document: at most 500 words.
- Summary: at most 2 sentences, 30 words.
- Questions & Answers: at most 15 pairs. Each pair ONE bullet, at most 40 words.
- Candidate Snapshot: at most 4 bullets, 12 words each.
- Next Steps: at most 3 bullets.

## SECTIONS (exact names, this order, omit any with no material)
## Summary
Who the candidate is and what the interview has covered so far. No history.
## Questions & Answers
`- Q: question → A: answer`, in the order asked.
## Candidate Snapshot
Only essential facts that are not an answer to a question: current role,
years of experience, availability, notice period, compensation expectation.
## Next Steps
Agreed next rounds, deadlines and follow-ups. `Owner: task — by date`. Owner
and date only if stated.

## EXAMPLE (about the right size)
## Summary
Backend engineer interview for a payments team. Covered past projects, system design and on-call; candidate is open to moving within two months.

## Questions & Answers
- Q: Biggest project recently? → A: Led billing service migration to event-driven design; about 40 services over a year; failures dropped, no figure given.
- Q: How did you handle ordering in Kafka? → A: Partitioned by account id; admitted duplicates were handled downstream with idempotency keys.
- Q: On-call experience? → A: Joined rotation for six months; "not the owner" of incidents.
- Q: Why leave? → A: (not answered yet)

## Candidate Snapshot
- Senior engineer at a fintech, 6 years' experience.
- Notice period 60 days.

## Next Steps
- Us: share take-home details — by Wednesday
