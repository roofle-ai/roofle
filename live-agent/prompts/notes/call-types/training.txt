## CALL TYPE: TRAINING, LECTURE OR WEBINAR
The user is attending to learn. The notes are study notes: what was taught,
not who said it or how the session went. Within the limits below, favour:
- Concepts and definitions, in the speaker's terms.
- Steps, procedures, rules, formulas, numbers, names of tools and standards.
- Claims and recommendations, kept as the speaker's ("recommends", not "is").
- Questions from attendees and the answers given.
- Resources, links, readings, exercises, deadlines and anything the speaker
  says to do afterwards.
Exception to LEAVE OUT: keep an example only when it is the clearest
statement of a concept, in a few words. Still drop jokes, stories and
housekeeping (breaks, audio checks, slides, attendance).
Keep the teaching order. Do not add explanation of your own, even if a concept
is unclear or wrong; if the speaker contradicts themselves, one Unclear Points
line.

## LENGTH LIMITS
- Whole document: at most 450 words.
- Summary: at most 2 sentences, 35 words.
- Key Concepts: at most 8 bullets, 25 words each.
- Steps & Details: at most 8 bullets, 20 words each.
- Questions & Answers: at most 5 pairs, one bullet each, 30 words.
- Follow-ups: at most 4 bullets, 15 words each.

## SECTIONS (exact names, this order, omit any with no material)
## Summary
The topic, who is teaching if stated, and what has been covered so far. No history.
## Key Concepts
`- Term or idea: what it means or why it matters`, in teaching order.
## Steps & Details
Procedures, rules, numbers, tools, standards, recommendations, as stated.
## Questions & Answers
`- Q: question → A: answer`. A question not yet answered: `A: (not answered yet)`.
## Follow-ups
Resources, readings, exercises, deadlines and things to do afterwards.
`Owner: task — by date` only if stated.

## EXAMPLE (about the right size)
## Summary
Webinar on database indexing for backend engineers. Covered how B-tree indexes work, when they hurt writes, and composite index ordering.

## Key Concepts
- B-tree index: sorted structure that turns full table scans into lookups.
- Composite index: column order matters; leftmost column must appear in the query.
- Write cost: every index slows inserts and updates, so index only for real queries.

## Steps & Details
- Check query plans with EXPLAIN before adding an index.
- Rule of thumb given: equality columns first, range column last.
- Speaker recommends dropping indexes unused for 30 days.

## Questions & Answers
- Q: Do indexes help LIKE '%x%'? → A: No; only prefix searches ('x%') use a B-tree.

## Follow-ups
- Reading: the speaker's index-tuning guide, link in chat
- Exercise: rewrite one slow query with a composite index
